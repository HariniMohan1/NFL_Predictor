EPA — Expected Points Added

Before every play, based on the down, yards to go, and field position, statisticians have calculated the average number of points a team is expected to score from that situation. EPA measures how much a play changed that expectation.

The EPA equation
Applied to every single play in the game
EPA = EP_after − EP_before where: EP_before = expected points BEFORE this play EP_after = expected points AFTER this play
EP_before
On 1st & 10 from your own 30-yard line, the historical average is about 1.3 points scored in the drive. So EP_before = 1.3
EP_after
If the play gains 20 yards, now it's 1st & 10 from the opponent's 50. Historical average there is about 2.8 points. EP_after = 2.8
EPA result
2.8 − 1.3 = +1.5 EPA. The play added 1.5 expected points. It was a good play.
Intuition
Think of EP as the "stock price" of your possession. A big gain = stock goes up = positive EPA. A sack = stock goes down = negative EPA. An interception = stock crashes to ~−4 EPA because you gave the ball away.
Play-by-play examples
+3.5
Touchdown pass
Ball thrown into end zone. Massive positive EPA — changes everything.
+1.8
40-yard completion
3rd & 8, gains the first down. Moves into field goal range.
+0.1
3-yard run
1st & 10, gains 3. Small positive — made some progress.
−1.2
Sack
QB tackled behind line. Loses yards and burns a down. Very costly.
−4.0
Interception
Ball stolen by defense. Opponent now has possession. Catastrophic.
−3.5
Fumble lost
Ball dropped and recovered by opponent. Similarly devastating.
Why we use EPA instead of points scored
Rolling EPA — our main feature
We take the average EPA per play over the last 4 weeks
roll_off_epa(team, week) = mean(EPA per play, weeks N-4 to N-1) shift(1) rule: week N cannot use week N's own data → strictly uses weeks N-4 through N-1 only
Positive value
Team is currently playing well offensively. Their plays are regularly exceeding expectations.
Near zero
Team is average — plays are meeting expectations but not exceeding them.
Negative value
Team is struggling. Their plays are regularly underperforming expectations — a sign of poor form.
Problem with points scored

A team can score 35 points against a terrible defense, or score 17 against a great defense. Points don't tell you how well the team actually played.

Why EPA is better

EPA per play removes the opponent effect. A +0.15 EPA per play means the team consistently gains more than expected regardless of who they face.

ELO Ratings

ELO is a system originally designed for chess. Every team gets a rating number. Win against a strong team → rating goes up a lot. Lose to a weak team → rating drops a lot. The rating captures years of accumulated team quality in one number.

ELO win probability equation
Before any game, we can calculate the expected win probability
P(home wins) = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_away − R_home − H) / 400)) where: R_home = home team's current ELO rating R_away = away team's current ELO rating H = home field advantage = +65 ELO points 400 = scaling constant (from original chess formula)
R_home − R_away
The rating gap. A 100-point gap means the stronger team wins ~64% of the time. A 200-point gap means ~76%.
+65 (home advantage)
Home teams win ~57% of NFL games. Adding 65 ELO points to the home team's effective rating captures this empirically.
10^(x/400)
The logistic curve. Ensures probability stays between 0% and 100% no matter how large the gap is.
Example
KC (ELO 1620) hosts BAL (ELO 1580) at home. Gap = 1620 − 1580 + 65 = 105. P(KC wins) = 1 / (1 + 10^(−105/400)) = 1 / (1 + 0.543) = 64.8%
ELO update equation
After every game, both teams' ratings update based on the result
R_new = R_old + K × (S − P_expected) where: K = 22 (how fast ratings change — the K-factor) S = actual result (1 = win, 0 = loss) P_expected = predicted win probability (from equation above)
S − P_expected
The surprise factor. If KC was expected to win 80% and did win, the surprise is only 0.20 — small rating gain. If KC was expected to win 80% and lost, surprise = −0.80 — large rating drop.
K = 22
NFL K-factor is higher than chess (6-10) because teams only play 17 games per year, so each game carries more information about real team quality.
Season regression
Each offseason: R_new = 1500 + 0.67 × (R_old − 1500). Moves 33% back toward 1500 to account for roster changes.
Example
KC (ELO 1620) beats BAL. Expected win = 64.8%, actual = 1 (win). Update = 22 × (1 − 0.648) = +7.7. KC new ELO = 1627.7. BAL loses −7.7 points → 1572.3

Monte Carlo Simulation

We know each team's probability of winning any single game. But to find the Super Bowl probability, we need to simulate the entire season + playoffs. Monte Carlo does this by repeating the full simulation 10,000 times and counting outcomes.

The name "Monte Carlo" comes from the famous casino in Monaco — because the method relies on randomness, just like gambling.

Win probability per game (the coin flip)
For each remaining game, we blend three signals
P(home wins) = 0.40 × P_elo + 0.40 × P_epa + 0.20 × P_to where: P_elo = win probability from ELO rating gap P_epa = win probability from rolling EPA differential P_to = win probability from turnover margin differential
P_elo (40%)
Historical team quality. Strong teams with high ELO deserve significant weight even if recent form is shaky.
P_epa (40%)
Current form. A team playing great football right now — high recent EPA — should get equal weight to history.
P_to (20%)
Turnover margin. Teams winning the turnover battle win ~80% of games. It gets its own weight because it is so predictive.
Super Bowl probability equation
SB_prob(team) = (wins across 10,000 simulations) / 10,000 × 100% For each simulation: 1. Simulate all remaining regular season games using P(home wins) 2. Sort teams by wins → seed playoff bracket (7 per conference) 3. Simulate: Wild Card → Divisional → Conference Champ → Super Bowl 4. Record which team won the Super Bowl 5. Repeat from step 1
Real result — 2025 season
SEA won the Super Bowl in 4,231 out of 10,000 simulations? No — 1,970 out of 10,000, giving 19.7%. With 32 teams competing, 19.7% is enormous. Random would be 3.1%.
Why 10,000 simulations?
100

Too noisy. Results jump around by 5-10% just from randomness.
1,000

Better. Results stable to ~1%. Takes under 1 second.

Brier Score — measuring prediction quality

Accuracy (right/wrong) does not tell the full story. A model that says "99% chance KC wins" when they barely win is technically correct but dangerously overconfident. Brier Score measures how calibrated the probabilities are.

Brier Score equation
Applied to each game prediction
Brier = (predicted_probability − actual_outcome)² where: predicted_probability = model's win probability (0.0 to 1.0) actual_outcome = 1 if team won, 0 if team lost
Lower = better
A perfect model with probability 1.0 for every winner scores 0.0. Always predicting 50% gives 0.25 (the random baseline).
The squaring
Squaring means overconfident wrong predictions are punished heavily. Predicting 95% and being wrong is much worse than predicting 60% and being wrong.
Our model score
0.2127 on the 2024 holdout season — better than the random baseline (0.25) and better than the ELO-only baseline (0.228).
Real 2025 Brier examples — SEA won Super Bowl LX

(0.80 − 1)² = 0.04

Confident AND correct. Near-perfect prediction.

Picked SEA at 40% confidence
(0.40 − 1)² = 0.36

Right team, but not confident enough.

User Carol — worst
Picked KC at 70% confidence
(0.70 − 0)² = 0.49

Wrong team AND overconfident. Harshly penalised.

Wrong team but honest about uncertainty. Beats Carol!

Dave was wrong but scores better than Carol — because he was honest that he was uncertain. Brier Score rewards calibration over boldness.

AUC — Area Under the ROC Curve
A second metric measuring discrimination power
AUC = P(model scores winner higher than loser) Range: 0.5 = random (coin flip) 0.7 = good discrimination 0.8 = excellent 1.0 = perfect Our model: AUC = 0.735 (2024 holdout season)
Plain English
Pick any game the home team won and any game the away team won. AUC of 0.735 means our model correctly assigns the higher win probability to the actual winner 73.5% of the time.
Why it matters
AUC works at every threshold simultaneously. It does not depend on choosing a cutoff like "predict win if probability > 50%". It measures pure ranking quality.

What our project does — step by step

We built an end-to-end system that takes raw NFL data and produces a probability for every team winning the Super Bowl. Here is how all the pieces connect.

Step 1 — Load data from nflverse
Download 373,381 plays, 60,788 injury records, 2,821 draft picks and 3,028 game results from the nflverse database. Cache as Parquet so it only downloads once.
Step 2 — Fit ELO ratings
Run through all games from 2015 to 2025. After each game, update both teams' ELO ratings using the update equation. By end of 2025: SEA = 1643 (highest), average = 1500.
Step 3 — Engineer 32 features per game
For every game, compute: elo_diff, home_win_prob_elo, rolling 4-week EPA (off, def, pass, rush), rolling turnover margin, sack rate, 3rd down rate, injury impact scores, draft quality scores. All using shift(1) to prevent leakage.
Step 4 — Train XGBoost on 2018-2023
XGBoost learns from 2,227 games. Using TimeSeriesSplit CV (5 folds, always future-only test sets) to tune hyperparameters. Then apply isotonic calibration to make probabilities honest.
Step 5 — Evaluate on 2024 holdout
Test on 285 games the model never saw. Result: 68.1% accuracy, Brier 0.2127, AUC 0.735. All three targets met. ELO baseline was 63% accuracy for comparison.
Step 6 — Run 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations
Simulate the full 2025 season 10,000 times. Each simulation: coin flip every game → sort by wins → seed playoffs → Wild Card → Divisional → Conf Champ → Super Bowl. Count winners.
Step 7 — Result: SEA ranked #1 at 19.7%
Out of 10,000 simulations, SEA won the Super Bowl in ~1,970 of them. The Seahawks won Super Bowl LX in real life, defeating the New England Patriots 29-13 on February 8, 2026.
All equations used in the project
ELO win probability
P = 1/(1+10^((Ra−Rh−65)/400))
Converts rating gap into win probability. Used for every game prediction and Monte Carlo.
ELO update
R_new = R_old + 22×(S − P)
Updates ratings after each game. K=22 for NFL. S=1 for win, 0 for loss.
Rolling EPA
mean(EPA, weeks N-4 to N-1)
4-week rolling average of EPA per play. shift(1) ensures no leakage from current week.
EPA per play
EPA = EP_after − EP_before
How much a play changed the expected scoring. Positive = good, negative = bad.
Injury impact score
Σ(pos_weight × severity)
Sum of position weight × injury severity for every injured player. QB=3.5, Out=1.0.
Monte Carlo probability
SB% = wins / 10,000 × 100
Count how many of 10,000 full playoff simulations each team wins.
Brier Score
Brier = (prob − outcome)²
Measures calibration quality. Lower is better. 0 = perfect, 0.25 = random.
Season regression
R = 1500 + 0.67×(R − 1500)
Applied each offseason to move ratings 33% toward the 1500 mean. Accounts for roster changes.
